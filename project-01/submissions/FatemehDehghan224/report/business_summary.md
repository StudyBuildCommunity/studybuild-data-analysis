# Business Summary

## Portfolio Overview

The portfolio contains 677,991 policies and 358,482.83 exposure years. It recorded 26,444 claims, giving an observed Claim Frequency of 0.0738 claims per exposure year. Total Claim Cost is €59.91 million and Average Severity is €2,265.51 per claim. These measures describe the observed portfolio and should be reviewed together.

## Key Regional Findings

Rhone-Alpes has the highest observed Claim Frequency at 0.0934, above the portfolio rate, with 45,343.55 exposure years and €10.28 million of Total Claim Cost. This makes it a useful regional monitoring priority. Centre has the largest Total Claim Cost (€19.07 million) and Exposure (102,707.85 years), but its Claim Frequency is below the portfolio rate at 0.0630. Portfolio scale therefore explains part of its total cost.

## Driver and Vehicle Findings

The Young (18-24) driver group has an observed Claim Frequency of 0.1610 and Average Severity of €5,838.56, both above the portfolio benchmarks. Among vehicle-age groups, Mid-age (6-10) vehicles have the highest observed Claim Frequency at 0.0809, while Old (11-20) vehicles have the highest Average Severity at €2,917.02. These are segment comparisons for monitoring, not evidence that an observed characteristic causes losses.

## Frequency vs Severity

Frequency and severity do not always move together. Diesel policies have higher observed Claim Frequency than Regular policies (0.0788 versus 0.0692), while Regular policies have higher Average Severity (€2,486.88 versus €2,051.65). Management should compare Claim Frequency and Average Severity with Exposure and Claim Count rather than relying on a single ranking.

## Claim Cost Concentration

Claim cost is concentrated in a small share of claims. The top 1% of observed claims account for 38.01% of Total Claim Cost. The 133 claims at or above €34,376.96, the 99.5th-percentile threshold, account for €19.74 million or 32.96% of Total Claim Cost.

## Unusual Claims

The largest observed claim is €4.08 million. High-cost claims should be reviewed for claim-file completeness, reserving, and operational themes. They should remain in reporting unless a specific validation identifies an error; an unusual value is not automatically an invalid claim.

## Recommendations

### Recommendation 1

**Evidence:** Young (18-24) drivers show higher observed Claim Frequency (0.1610) and Average Severity (€5,838.56) than the portfolio.

**Action:** Add this cohort to the recurring frequency-and-severity review. Compare claim mix, vehicle mix, and exposure composition before proposing an intervention.

**KPI:** Claim Frequency, Average Severity, Exposure, and Claim Count for Young (18-24) drivers.

### Recommendation 2

**Evidence:** Rhone-Alpes has the highest observed regional Claim Frequency (0.0934) and €10.28 million of Total Claim Cost.

**Action:** Include Rhone-Alpes in the monthly regional review and compare its claims mix and claims handling with portfolio benchmarks.

**KPI:** Claim Frequency, Total Claim Cost, Average Severity, Exposure, and Claim Count for Rhone-Alpes.

### Recommendation 3

**Evidence:** The top 1% of claims contribute 38.01% of Total Claim Cost.

**Action:** Maintain a high-cost claim review queue for claim-file validation, reserving, and recurring operational themes without automatically excluding valid observations.

**KPI:** Claim Cost Concentration, count of claims above the 99.5th-percentile threshold, and Total Claim Cost reviewed.

## Limitations

- **Historical data:** the portfolio covers approximately 2011–2013 and may not reflect current claims patterns, costs, regulation, or vehicle technology.
- **Anonymous insurer:** the insurer is not identified, so underwriting strategy, coverage terms, and claims practices cannot be evaluated.
- **No premium information:** written premium is unavailable. Therefore, Loss Ratio and profitability cannot be calculated.
- **Limited risk variables:** the dataset omits factors that may influence outcomes, including detailed driving behaviour, journey purpose, coverage terms, and claims-process information.
- **Descriptive analytics:** observed relationships are associations, not proof of causation. They do not show that a segment characteristic caused an outcome.
- Small groups can produce volatile rates. Claim Count and Exposure should be considered before escalating a result.
- The claim-level data has no insurer-issued claim identifier and does not determine claim validity, root cause, or preventability.
